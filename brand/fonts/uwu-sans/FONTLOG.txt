FONTLOG for UwU Sans
====================

UwU Sans is the interface font of UwUMail. It is a Modified Version of
Atkinson Hyperlegible Next (Braille Institute of America, designed by
Applied Design Works and Letters from Sweden), licensed under the SIL Open
Font License 1.1 (see OFL.txt). The upstream license names no Reserved Font
Name; the family is renamed anyway so it can never be mistaken for the
original.

ChangeLog
---------
2026-10-02  Version 1.000 (MinifyX)
- Renamed: family "UwU Sans", PostScript "UwUSans-Regular", variations
  prefix "UwUSans", unique ID "1.000;MFX;UwUSans-Regular", vendor "MFX".
  Copyright line for the modification added, upstream copyright kept.
- Subset to Latin, Latin Extended, combining marks, general punctuation,
  currency, letterlike symbols and the math signs upstream ships (upstream
  has nothing outside these ranges, so no letters were lost). Hinting
  instructions dropped (upstream variable font is unhinted in practice).
- Italic not included; browsers slant the roman when needed.
- New glyphs, drawn as code in build.py, with gvar deltas at wght 200 and
  800 so their strokes follow the weight axis like the letters:
  - nyu (U+E000, Private Use): Nyu, a cat face. Round head with two ears in
    one stroke, oval eyes, a ":3" mouth. Sits on the baseline, ears reach
    about cap height.
  - heart (U+2665 BLACK HEART SUIT). U+2764 is left out on purpose so the
    colour emoji keeps winning for ❤.
  - arrowleft/up/right/down (U+2190-2193): upstream has no arrows, the
    UwUMail interface uses them.
- New `calt` lookup: ":3" becomes nyu, "<3" becomes heart, only when no
  letter or digit stands directly before the ":"/"<" or directly after the
  "3". Times (10:30), comparisons (x<3, 1<35) and words stay untouched.
  Works with `tnum` active. Apps switch it off in inputs with
  `font-variant-ligatures: no-contextual`.
- Letters, digits, spacing and kerning are unchanged. Tabular figures come
  from upstream's `tnum`. No tracking baked in; the app sets letter-spacing
  in CSS.
